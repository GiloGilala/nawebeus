\## 1. Overview



This document defines the end-to-end user journeys for \*\*Nawebeus\*\* — a unified social media and PR management platform built for the African market. User journeys map the complete experience of a user from initial discovery through ongoing usage, including onboarding, core workflows, and long-term retention.



These journeys are living documents that guide product design, feature prioritization, UX optimization, engineering sequencing, and customer success strategy. They are grounded in persona research and updated as the platform evolves.



Each journey includes:



\- \*\*Persona:\*\* Who is taking this journey and why

\- \*\*Journey Stages:\*\* Step-by-step progression through the experience

\- \*\*Touchpoints:\*\* Where and how the user interacts with the platform

\- \*\*Emotions:\*\* How the user feels at each stage

\- \*\*Pain Points:\*\* What currently frustrates the user in this workflow

\- \*\*Nawebeus Solution:\*\* How the platform improves the experience

\- \*\*Success Metrics:\*\* How we measure whether the journey is working

\- \*\*Financial Context:\*\* Where relevant, cost and value considerations in Nigerian Naira (₦)

\## 6. Journey 4: Crisis Detection \& Response



\### 6.1 Journey Overview



| Field | Details |

|-------|---------|

| \*\*Primary Personas\*\* | Ngozi (Crisis Manager), Ade (Head of PR) |

| \*\*Supporting Persona\*\* | Chidi (Head of Marketing) |

| \*\*Entry Point\*\* | Automated crisis alert, anomalous sentiment spike, unusual mention volume increase |

| \*\*Goal\*\* | Detect a brand crisis at least 2 hours before it reaches mass public awareness, and execute a coordinated, effective response within 15 minutes of detection |

| \*\*Frequency\*\* | Infrequent but extremely high stakes when it occurs |

| \*\*Business Stakes\*\* | A poorly handled social media crisis can cost a Nigerian brand ₦1,000,000,000+ in market capitalization, regulatory fines, and customer attrition |



\---



\### 6.2 Journey Stages



| Stage | User Action | Touchpoint | User Emotion | Current Pain Point | Nawebeus Solution |

|-------|-------------|------------|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|

| \*\*Signal Detection\*\* | System identifies unusual patterns in mention volume, sentiment, or reach | AI monitoring engine, anomaly detection algorithm | — (automated) | Existing tools detect volume spikes but not early-stage sentiment anomalies | Machine learning anomaly detection that identifies emerging crises from weak signals — unusual sentiment clustering before volume spikes |

| \*\*Alert Receipt\*\* | Ngozi or Ade receives a crisis alert | Push notification (mobile), email alert, in-app alert, SMS | Alarmed — heart rate increases immediately | Alerts arrive as generic notifications: \*"New mentions found"\* — no context on severity | Structured crisis alert: Severity level (1–5), what is happening, where it is spreading, estimated current reach, projected reach in 2 hours, and recommended immediate action |

| \*\*Rapid Assessment\*\* | User opens the crisis brief and assesses the situation | Crisis command view, AI-generated crisis brief | Under pressure — must decide quickly with imperfect information | User must manually search across platforms to understand the scope of the crisis | Auto-generated crisis brief: source of origin, spreading channels, key voices amplifying the story, sentiment breakdown, historical precedents for similar crises, and recommended severity classification |

| \*\*Severity Classification\*\* | User confirms or adjusts the AI-suggested severity level | Severity selector, justification field | Decisive — committing to a course of action | No standardized severity framework; different people make different judgments | 5-level severity framework with clear definitions: S1 (Noise), S2 (Monitor), S3 (Respond), S4 (Escalate), S5 (All-hands crisis) — AI pre-classifies, human confirms |

| \*\*Stakeholder Activation\*\* | System or user activates the appropriate stakeholder notification workflow | Stakeholder workflow, notification templates | Coordinated but urgent | Manual WhatsApp messages to CEO, legal, and communications director — no audit trail, no consistency | Pre-configured stakeholder notification workflows by severity level: S3 triggers automatic notification to Head of PR and Legal; S4 adds CEO and CFO; S5 activates the full crisis management team with pre-drafted talking points |

| \*\*Response Drafting\*\* | User selects and customizes a response template | Response template library, AI writing assistant | Focused — words must be perfect under time pressure | Writing a response from scratch under extreme pressure leads to errors and delays | Template library organized by crisis type (product failure, regulatory issue, social backlash, data breach, executive misconduct) with pre-approved language reviewed by legal; AI drafts the first version in seconds |

| \*\*Response Approval\*\* | Response goes through expedited approval workflow | Emergency approval workflow, mobile approval | Tense — waiting for approval while the crisis grows | Normal approval workflows take 24–48 hours; no emergency track exists | Emergency approval workflow: pre-designated crisis approvers receive mobile push notification with one-tap approve/reject; SLA of 5 minutes |

| \*\*Multi-Channel Response Execution\*\* | Approved response is published across all relevant channels simultaneously | Multi-channel publisher, distribution tracking | Relieved — action is being taken | Logging into each platform separately to post the response loses precious minutes | One-click multi-channel distribution: Twitter/X, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, press wire, and internal communications simultaneously |

| \*\*Real-Time Sentiment Monitoring\*\* | User monitors how the response is being received | Live sentiment dashboard, response tracking | Watchful — has the response helped or made it worse? | No way to measure response effectiveness in real time | Live response impact tracking: sentiment shift post-response, reach of response vs. reach of crisis content, influencer reaction monitoring |

| \*\*Post-Crisis Analysis\*\* | User reviews the full incident and extracts lessons | Post-crisis incident report, timeline reconstruction | Exhausted but professional — must document this properly | Post-crisis analysis is manual, incomplete, and often skipped due to time pressure | Auto-generated post-crisis incident report: complete timeline, media spread map, sentiment recovery curve, response effectiveness score, and recommended process improvements |



\---



\### 6.3 Crisis Severity Framework



| Level | Name | Definition | Example | Response SLA | Stakeholders Notified |

|-------|------|-----------|---------|-------------|----------------------|

| \*\*S1\*\* | Noise | Isolated negative mentions with no spread potential | One angry customer tweet | Monitor only | None |

| \*\*S2\*\* | Watch | Growing negative sentiment; potential to escalate | 20+ negative mentions in 2 hours | Review within 4 hours | Head of PR |

| \*\*S3\*\* | Respond | Active negative narrative forming; media picking up | Journalist asking for comment on negative story | Respond within 1 hour | Head of PR, Legal, Head of Marketing |

| \*\*S4\*\* | Escalate | Crisis is trending; significant brand damage occurring | Story trending on Twitter/X with 500+ mentions | Respond within 15 minutes | CEO, CFO, Legal, Head of PR, Head of Marketing |

| \*\*S5\*\* | All-Hands | Full-blown crisis; national media coverage | Front-page story, regulatory inquiry, major financial impact | Immediate — crisis room activated | Full executive team, Board communications |

| \*\*AI-Assisted Optimization\*\* | Bola uses AI suggestions to improve copy engagement | AI writing assistant, engagement predictor | Curious — does this suggestion actually help? | No data-driven feedback on whether copy will perform before publishing | AI engagement score with specific improvement suggestions: \*"Adding a question increases engagement by 23% for this audience"\* |

| \*\*Publishing Execution\*\* | Posts go live on schedule | Publishing queue, real-time status monitor | Watchful — checking that posts actually went live | Posts occasionally fail silently; Bola discovers failures when followers point them out | Real-time publishing status with immediate failure alerts and automatic retry logic; failure notification within 60 seconds with reason and resolution suggestion |

\## 8. Journey 6: Engagement \& Response



\### 8.1 Journey Overview



| Field | Details |

|-------|---------|

| \*\*Primary Personas\*\* | Bola (Social Media Manager), Chidi (Head of Marketing) |

| \*\*Entry Point\*\* | Unified inbox notification, real-time mention alert, scheduled community management session |

| \*\*Goal\*\* | Respond to all audience messages, comments, and mentions within SLA, with consistent brand voice and appropriate personalization |

| \*\*Frequency\*\* | Multiple times per day, 7 days per week |

| \*\*Business Stakes\*\* | Unanswered customer complaints on social media in Nigeria frequently escalate to Twitter/X threads that attract media attention; a 2-hour SLA breach on a complaint from a verified account can lead to viral negative coverage |



\---



\### 8.2 Journey Stages



| Stage | User Action | Touchpoint | User Emotion | Current Pain Point | Nawebeus Solution |

|-------|-------------|------------|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|

| \*\*Inbox Review\*\* | Bola opens the unified inbox and scans new messages | Unified inbox, priority filter, platform tabs | Alert — scanning for anything urgent | Opens 5 separate platform apps; missed messages discovered hours later | Single unified inbox aggregating DMs, comments, mentions, and tagged posts from all platforms; unread count by platform visible at a glance |

| \*\*Priority Triage\*\* | Sorts messages by urgency: complaint vs. compliment vs. question vs. crisis signal | Priority classifier, filter panel, labels | Decisive — needs to act on the right messages first | Manual reading of every message to determine priority — slow and error-prone | AI-powered message classification: Complaint, Question, Compliment, Crisis Signal, Spam — with priority scoring based on sender influence and sentiment |

\## 15. Cross-Journey Patterns and Insights



\### 15.1 Cross-Journey Behavioral Patterns



| Pattern | Description | Journeys Affected | Design Implication |

|---------|-------------|------------------|-------------------|

| \*\*Alert-Driven Urgency\*\* | Users are pulled into the platform by time-sensitive alerts rather than scheduled sessions | Crisis Detection, Media Monitoring, Engagement | Alerts must be immediately actionable; the platform must be operable under high stress in under 60 seconds |

| \*\*Batch Work Preference\*\* | Power users prefer to do all publishing, reviewing, and planning in concentrated sessions rather than continuously | Social Publishing, Content Strategy | Bulk scheduling, batch approval, and weekly planning workflows must be first-class features |

| \*\*Reporting as a Ritual\*\* | Report generation has a fixed weekly and monthly cadence that users structure their work around | Analytics, PR Relations, Agency Management | Automation of the reporting ritual eliminates the largest recurring time cost across all personas |

| \*\*Competitive Awareness as Status\*\* | For senior personas, knowing competitor activity is a professional identity need, not just a nice-to-have | Competitive Intelligence, Media Monitoring | Competitive data must be prominent, not buried; it is used in executive conversations and client meetings |

| \*\*Mobile for Crisis, Desktop for Strategy\*\* | Senior personas switch to mobile exclusively during crises; all strategic work happens on desktop | Crisis Detection, Media Monitoring | Mobile experience must prioritize speed and simplicity; desktop experience can be feature-rich |
